# Critique

How to analyze an artifact's weaknesses without proposing fixes.

## Process

1. **Read the artifact** — understand it thoroughly before critiquing
2. **Analyze weaknesses** across these dimensions:
   - **Accuracy** — factual errors, unsupported claims, logical gaps
   - **Completeness** — missing coverage, unexplored angles, gaps in argument
   - **Clarity** — confusing passages, ambiguous language, poor structure
   - **Coherence** — contradictions within the piece, inconsistent framing
   - **Domain-specific** — whatever quality criteria the domain context specifies
3. **Rank weaknesses by severity** — most impactful first
4. **Write the critique** — specific, actionable identification of problems

## Rules

- **Identify, don't fix**. Your job is to find what's wrong, not to rewrite it.
- **Be specific**. "The argument is weak" is useless. "The claim in paragraph 3 that X causes Y is unsupported — no evidence is cited" is useful.
- **Be honest, not harsh**. The goal is improvement, not demolition.
- **Don't propose alternatives**. That's the author's job with fresh context.
- **Focus on substance, not style** — unless style actively undermines clarity.

## Output

Write your critique to paw-fs. It should be structured by severity with specific references to passages in the artifact.
