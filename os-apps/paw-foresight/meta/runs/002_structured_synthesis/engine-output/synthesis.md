# Foresight Projection: Directed Software Evolution v2

## Executive Summary

Anthropic Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, and Cursor appear headed toward broad enterprise embedding, but the durable product is not a free-form autonomous engineer. The durable product is the repository harness bundle: deterministic setup, replay tests, policy checks, and promotion gates that let agents touch 35% or more of low-to-medium risk tickets in frontier firms while machine-checkable gates decide most AI-assisted pull requests [obs: en-019d9494-8057-7051-9312-c5f657b62dae] [obs: en-019d9491-0e29-7652-a06e-a0ea5858d699]. Temper, Cedar, OPA, Kubernetes, and Terraform matter here because they expose machine-usable state and policy surfaces rather than raw shell access [obs: en-019d9491-0e38-78b1-aea1-b2eef3ff8080] [obs: en-019d9494-8066-7280-a27e-8bec0c6b00a1].

The strongest negative finding is that benchmark progress from Cognition Devin, SWE-agent derivatives, and related systems still transfers poorly into messy production environments. Two quantitative ceilings recur in the observation set: fewer than 15% of benchmark-style autonomous task wins transfer cleanly to unattended enterprise execution in weak environments, and fewer than 30% of multi-step workflows that cross CI, infra, and deploy boundaries finish without human unblock actions [obs: en-019d9491-0e54-72c3-b431-b177e5c03e67] [obs: en-019d9494-8083-7920-95b0-7338fd5be8b7]. That is why safety policy is scaling faster than liveness: more than 70% of enterprise agent policies are projected to encode prohibitions while fewer than 20% encode explicit progress guarantees [obs: en-019d9491-0e62-79d1-bc14-b455d09a39e8].

The compounding advantage therefore shifts away from raw model intelligence and toward portfolio search plus evolutionary memory. Organizations that retain telemetry, incident traces, rollback outcomes, and rejected attempts are projected to improve autonomous remediation quality by roughly 2x versus trace-poor peers, while leading teams increasingly run 5 to 10 candidate patches or runbooks in parallel for high-value tasks [obs: en-019d9491-0e88-7a72-a0fe-6dddf65fa551] [obs: en-019d9494-80af-7781-8a34-aee474b5a5f6] [obs: en-019d9494-80be-7092-861d-1c44db0bc460]. The adjacent surprise is organizational: approval topology behaves like market structure, and firms that centralize signoff cap cycle-time gains near 30 to 40% even when models improve [obs: en-019d9494-80cb-73b2-8fc5-4f2ba8a422ba].

## Key Findings

1. **Anthropic Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, and Cursor shift enterprise adoption toward repository harness bundles rather than free-form autonomy**
   - Evidence: "By Q2 2027, Anthropic Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, and Cursor are embedded in standard engineering workflows at frontier software firms, but the durable unit of adoption is the repository harness bundle" [obs: en-019d9494-8057-7051-9312-c5f657b62dae]
   - Measurable indicator: 35%+ of low-to-medium risk tickets touched by agents without synchronous human editing

2. **Kubernetes, Terraform, and Cedar-backed control planes become the first durable machine-tool surface for software evolution**
   - Evidence: "Kubernetes, Terraform, and Cedar-backed control planes are the first durable machine-tool targets because their desired state is already declarative" [obs: en-019d9491-0e38-78b1-aea1-b2eef3ff8080]
   - Measurable indicator: 25%+ of new internal platform workflows generated from schemas, policies, or state-machine descriptions within 90 days

3. **Cognition Devin and SWE-agent-style systems keep showing a benchmark-to-production transfer gap**
   - Evidence: "fewer than 15% of seemingly successful autonomous task runs will transfer cleanly from benchmark-style evaluation to unattended enterprise execution" [obs: en-019d9491-0e54-72c3-b431-b177e5c03e67]
   - Measurable indicator: <15% clean transfer rate from benchmark-style success to unattended enterprise execution

4. **OPA, Cedar, and GitHub protections make prohibition easy but liveness hard**
   - Evidence: "more than 70% of enterprise agent policies will encode prohibitions while fewer than 20% encode explicit progress guarantees" [obs: en-019d9491-0e62-79d1-bc14-b455d09a39e8]
   - Measurable indicator: >70% prohibition-heavy policies vs <20% policies with explicit progress guarantees

5. **OpenAI-, Anthropic-, and Cursor-based workflows evolve toward portfolio search, not one-shot generation**
   - Evidence: "leading teams routinely run 5 to 10 candidate patches or runbooks" [obs: en-019d9494-80be-7092-861d-1c44db0bc460]
   - Measurable indicator: 5-10 candidate implementations evaluated for a high-value task

6. **Telemetry-rich firms gain a compounding edge because evolutionary memory beats raw model upgrades alone**
   - Evidence: "repositories with 12 months of retained traces and rollback outcomes improve autonomous remediation quality by roughly 2x" [obs: en-019d9494-80af-7781-8a34-aee474b5a5f6]
   - Measurable indicator: ~2x remediation quality improvement for trace-rich firms versus trace-poor peers

7. **Temper-like governed app platforms become practical because they replace shell-driven ops with policy-backed entities**
   - Evidence: "Temper-like governed app platforms gain practical traction for internal ops" [obs: en-019d9494-8066-7280-a27e-8bec0c6b00a1]
   - Measurable indicator: 10 production workflows per enterprise migrated into governed entities within 12 months

8. **Approval topology, not just model quality, determines whether dark-factory gains stall at 30-40%**
   - Evidence: "firms that centralize signoff with a few staff engineers cap cycle-time gains near 30 to 40%" [obs: en-019d9494-80cb-73b2-8fc5-4f2ba8a422ba]
   - Measurable indicator: 30-40% cycle-time ceiling under centralized approval

## Temporal Progression

### 6-9 months
- Repository-specific harnesses increasingly get packaged as reusable app workflows with state, policy, and auditable actions, raising routine end-to-end automation in platform work [obs: en-019d9492-b547-77f0-b28e-b8eb987d73a2] [obs: en-019d9492-b556-7501-a56c-bbef33f3aba9].

### 9-12 months
- Year-ahead adoption centers on governed harness bundles, 10-workflow internal app catalogs, and trace-backed operational memory rather than free-form model autonomy [obs: en-019d9494-8057-7051-9312-c5f657b62dae] [obs: en-019d9494-8066-7280-a27e-8bec0c6b00a1] [obs: en-019d9494-80af-7781-8a34-aee474b5a5f6].

## Active Directions

#### Near-term directed software evolution stalls at governed homeostasis before it reaches true dark-factory evolution
**Direction ID:** en-019d948f-8f70-7de3-a85f-8231ec2da58a

Over the next 90 days, the field will discover that governed autonomy scales more slowly than agent demos imply. The practical ceiling is not whether Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, Devin, or similar systems can write acceptable patches in isolation. It is whether organizations can define bounded action spaces, reversible workflows, and policy-approved escalation paths across repos, CI, IAM, deployment, and incident response. This produces a structural asymmetry: safety controls mature first because they are easier to specify and audit, while liveness remains under-modeled. The result is a wave of systems that are good at stopping bad actions and recovering to known states, but weak at making ambitious, compounding improvements without human arbitration.

That asymmetry matters because directed evolution needs more than safe mutation. It needs trustworthy fitness functions, diversity-preserving search, and fast institutional permission to try nontrivial variants. In the next quarter, most teams will not have those ingredients. They will have benchmark harnesses, replay suites, branch protections, and policy engines, but not enough production-grounded selection pressure to distinguish a truly better architecture from one that merely passes existing checks. So the near-term winning pattern is not full dark-factory evolution; it is narrow autonomy inside heavily governed machine-tool platforms, with human authority concentrated around objective-setting, exception handling, and policy updates. Platforms like Temper are directionally right, but the constraint will be governance and liveness design, not raw generation quality.

Supporting observations: [obs: en-019d948f-8f24-7e62-9584-f49ab8c8c718], [obs: en-019d948f-8f33-73c0-98c3-8ad90b4ec08f], [obs: en-019d948f-8f41-7c42-bd7d-358222e210a2], [obs: en-019d948f-8f50-76e3-9685-f35c52f562ff], [obs: en-019d948f-8f5f-7901-b66c-01ce0325588a]

**Counterfactual:** If this thesis is wrong, organizations will demonstrate policy-compliant autonomous systems that not only avoid bad states but also ship materially better architectures and operational practices without governance becoming the binding constraint.

#### Selection infrastructure, not raw model intelligence, becomes the decisive bottleneck in directed software evolution
**Direction ID:** en-019d948f-9a6c-79a2-9236-c2d041f60099

From an adjacent-domain perspective, the next 90 days will make directed software evolution look less like a single AI capability race and more like the emergence of a new selective regime. In biology, rapid variation does not guarantee adaptation; the decisive factor is the environment that selects among variants. In economics, firms do not win because they can generate more options, but because they can price, test, and reallocate faster under local information. The same pattern is now appearing in software. As coding agents from Anthropic, OpenAI, Cursor, Aider, and related systems make mutation cheap, the bottleneck shifts to selection infrastructure: observability, replay, policy gates, state-machine constraints, and production-faithful evals. Organizations with those assets will compound, while those relying on broad benchmark improvements will see noisy gains and brittle deployments.

The strongest implication is institutional, not just technical. Directed evolution will first succeed where software behaves like governed infrastructure: internal platforms, deployment control planes, incident remediation, schema and policy management, and other domains with crisp invariants. These are the equivalent of stable ecological niches or tightly regulated markets. In those settings, Temper-like machine-tool systems can preserve diversity, enforce guardrails, and permit local exploration without catastrophic downside. By contrast, open-ended product logic will remain harder because its fitness function is socially negotiated and only partially visible to machines. So the winning strategy over the next quarter is to build richer multi-objective harnesses and treat governance as part of the adaptive engine, not as external bureaucracy.

**Counterfactual:** If this thesis is wrong, then frontier model improvements alone should drive broad autonomous software gains even in organizations with weak observability, weak policy controls, and poor replay infrastructure.

#### Harness stacks, not fully autonomous agents, will be the real adoption wedge for directed software evolution in the next 90 days.
**Direction ID:** en-019d948f-a603-70b2-81ec-99e3906006a9

Over the next 90 days, the winning implementation pattern for directed software evolution will be repo-native harness stacks rather than fully autonomous software engineers. Practitioner teams will standardize a pipeline that looks like this: repository bootstrap script, deterministic task fixture, layered verification cascade, ephemeral execution sandbox, and policy-gated promotion. Cursor, Claude Code, Codex-style agents, Aider, and similar tools will be retained where they can operate against those artifacts, because that lets teams convert model capability into governed throughput instead of unmanaged code churn. The practical shift is from prompting for code to specifying executable acceptance conditions.

This matters because harness quality compounds. Once a team has replayable failing cases, invariant checks, migration rehearsals, and CI-visible acceptance bundles, the same substrate supports human engineers, copilots, autonomous repair loops, and eventually variation-and-selection systems. The organizations that move first will not be those with the strongest frontier-model access alone; they will be the ones whose platform teams can package local evals, traces, and promotion gates into a standard developer workflow. The biggest mistake in this window is over-investing in generalized autonomy before making repository reality legible to agents.

**Counterfactual:** If general-purpose autonomous agents begin reliably handling repo-specific build, test, migration, and rollout workflows without heavy harness investment, then platform teams will skip the harness-first phase and adoption will centralize around model vendors rather than repository-local engineering discipline.

#### Directed software evolution consolidates around governed portfolio search, where archives and selection institutions matter more than raw generation.
**Direction ID:** en-019d9492-9d3f-7e42-b258-544a0d174000

Across economics, biology, and organizational theory, the durable pattern at one year is that directed software evolution becomes a governed search institution rather than a smarter autocomplete layer. Systems that win are those that formalize variation, selection, retention, and promotion: many candidate mutations are generated in parallel, fitness is assessed through local harnesses and policy gates, losers are archived for learning value, and only a small frontier set is promoted into production pathways. This resembles venture portfolio construction, evolutionary adaptation under explicit selection pressure, and the way mature organizations build routines to absorb experimentation without losing control.

The hidden bottleneck is therefore not raw model creativity but organizational capacity to encode selection pressure. Enterprises that build archives of failed attempts, maintain domain-specific evaluators, and decentralize approvals into policy-backed action classes get compounding returns because every run sharpens the search landscape. Enterprises that keep centralized approval queues and weak archives experience apparent technical stagnation: their agents generate possibilities, but the organization cannot metabolize them. Over the next year, the strategic moat shifts toward firms that own evaluation memory, promotion criteria, and adaptive governance for bounded autonomy.

**Counterfactual:** If this thesis is wrong, raw model improvements and single-agent UX will dominate, and archives, evaluators, and approval redesign will remain secondary implementation details rather than sources of strategic advantage.

#### Directed software evolution plateaus as governed optimization, not broad self-sustaining autonomy
**Direction ID:** en-019d9492-a722-7ca3-aea4-7194c96fa881

The critic case after one year is that directed software evolution has real local wins but fails its strongest narrative: organizations do not obtain broad, compounding, unattended self-improvement across production software estates. What scales is governed candidate generation, selective automation, and rich telemetry around human decisions. What does not scale is the claim that better models plus policy wrappers are sufficient to overcome approval drag, weak transfer from evals to production, and the unresolved problem of proving that long-horizon adaptations remain beneficial outside the harness that selected them.

The evidence pattern is adverse to maximalist claims. Governance tooling improves because enterprises need it, yet every new approval surface also documents where trust stops. Benchmarks rise while deployment confidence does not rise proportionally, especially in heterogeneous repos and infrastructure stacks. Systems appear more adaptive largely because teams are adding memory, routing, and review structure, not because the agents can safely mutate themselves or their environments over long horizons without supervision. The likely one-year equilibrium is therefore a plateau: directed evolution becomes an important design pattern for bounded engineering workflows, but the domain is still not ready to substantiate strong claims of autonomous, durable, organization-wide software evolution.

**Counterfactual:** If this thesis is wrong, approval structures relax faster than expected, production-grade evals generalize across real repositories, and organizations begin to demonstrate unattended adaptation with measurable long-run gains rather than bounded workflow optimization.

#### Year-ahead winners operationalize governed portfolio execution on app substrates, not general free-form coding agents
**Direction ID:** en-019d9492-b587-7031-9fe4-308ce7941738

Within one year, practitioners adopt agent systems where the unit of deployment is a governed workflow backed by typed state, policy checks, and deterministic evaluation rather than a chat session with shell access. The practical reason is operational economics: platform teams can approve expansion when actions are auditable, rollback surfaces are explicit, and success criteria are machine-checkable. That favors app-style platforms, declarative control planes, and repository harnesses that convert vague requests into bounded state transitions.

The strongest throughput gains come from portfolio execution. Instead of trusting one generated patch or plan, mature teams run several candidates through private eval suites, integration tests, replay fixtures, and policy gates, then promote the best surviving artifact. This is technically feasible now for maintenance, platform, and control-plane work because latency and compute costs are acceptable when constrained to high-leverage workflows. The result is not universal autonomy: greenfield product work remains human-led, but a growing share of repetitive production change flows crosses the threshold into low-touch autonomous operation.

#### By year-end, governed harness bundles become the default packaging layer for enterprise coding autonomy
**Direction ID:** en-019d9494-80d9-7c93-9ab7-7d3e19164a74

The first year does not end with unconstrained autonomous engineers replacing software teams. It ends with Anthropic Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, Cursor, Kubernetes, Terraform, and Temper-like workflow systems being stitched together through governed harness bundles. Those bundles include deterministic environment bootstrap, replay tests, migration rehearsal, policy checks, and promotion gates. Once those artifacts exist, the same repository can support humans, copilots, repair loops, and comparative candidate search. That makes the harness bundle, not the frontier model alone, the durable locus of value creation.

The practical implication is packaging. Enterprises will buy model access, but they will operationalize autonomy through repo-local machine-tooling that converts vague coding ability into legible, auditable work. Teams that invest in harness bundles achieve meaningful unattended throughput in low-to-medium risk tasks, while teams chasing generalized autonomy without repository structure remain trapped in demo mode. Governed app substrates gain from this because they let organizations express approval logic, rollback boundaries, and action surfaces in software rather than in oral tradition.

#### The year-one limit on dark-factory software is liveness debt, not safety debt
**Direction ID:** en-019d9494-80ed-7af3-bf5d-336129318b5a

After a year of experimentation, the pattern is clearer: OPA, Cedar, branch protections, sandboxing, and auditable environments can reduce unsafe actions, but they do not guarantee completion. The most expensive failures are stalled migrations, half-finished incident remediations, and autonomous workflows that gather evidence but cannot legally or procedurally cross the final boundary into execution. That makes liveness debt the hidden tax on dark-factory software.

This matters strategically because many organizations will misread safer agents as more capable agents. In reality, they have built better brakes, not better end-to-end operational systems. Enterprises that redesign approval topology and encode completion paths into governed workflows will keep compounding. Enterprises that only accumulate prohibitions will end up with fleets of polite, well-instrumented agents waiting for a human to finish the task.

**Counterfactual:** If liveness debt is not the main limit, then multi-step autonomous workflows should complete across repo, infra, and deploy boundaries at high rates under existing governance structures.

#### Directed software evolution becomes a memory-rich portfolio search discipline before it becomes a universal autonomous engineer
**Direction ID:** en-019d9494-8103-79d0-9f25-0255e31dc851

One year out, the strongest teams do not rely on single-shot agent answers. They run memory-rich portfolio search. OpenAI, Anthropic, Cursor, and internal systems generate multiple candidate patches, runbooks, or policy variants; telemetry, incident traces, rollback outcomes, and cost metrics then select what survives. This is closer to managed evolution than to task-completion theater. The compounding asset is not merely a good model, but a retained memory of which variants succeeded in which environments.

That creates a durable advantage for organizations that preserve diversity and evolutionary memory. Multiple candidates keep search from collapsing into the first acceptable patch, while long-lived traces keep the environment informative enough to distinguish robust gains from benchmark overfitting. Over time, the field will recognize that software autonomy scales with selection quality and memory depth more than with raw generation volume alone.

**Counterfactual:** If memory-rich portfolio search is not the winning pattern, then one-shot generation systems should match or exceed the performance of candidate-comparison systems in production operations over time.

## What Surprised Us

- Public benchmark progress turned out to be much less predictive than repo-specific replay fidelity, so local harness realism mattered more than leaderboard movement [obs: en-019d948f-a5f4-7e00-ba23-b4833be147e9].
- The main year-one failure mode was stalled completion rather than bad code generation, despite heavy investment in safety policy and sandboxing [obs: en-019d9494-8083-7920-95b0-7338fd5be8b7].
- Approval topology emerged as a harder limit than model IQ, capping gains near 30-40% in centralized organizations [obs: en-019d9494-80cb-73b2-8fc5-4f2ba8a422ba].
- The most strategic asset was not a better model but twelve months of telemetry, incidents, and rollback traces that improved remediation quality by about 2x [obs: en-019d9494-80af-7781-8a34-aee474b5a5f6].
- Directed evolution looked more like governed portfolio search than like a single autonomous engineer replacing teams [obs: en-019d9494-80be-7092-861d-1c44db0bc460].

## Assumptions & Limitations

1. **Assumption:** Anthropic, OpenAI, Cursor, and related vendors continue improving tool-use reliability faster than enterprises improve trust.
   - **If-wrong:** Broad autonomous coding may stall even in well-harnessed environments.
   - **Confidence:** medium

2. **Assumption:** Enterprises are willing to encode workflows in Cedar-, OPA-, or Temper-style governed systems rather than keeping ops informal.
   - **If-wrong:** Auditability improves slowly and shell-centric operations remain dominant longer.
   - **Confidence:** medium

3. **Assumption:** Private telemetry, incident traces, and rollback archives remain available for evolutionary memory and do not get blocked by data-retention, privacy, or organizational fragmentation.
   - **If-wrong:** The projected 2x advantage for trace-rich firms will not materialize.
   - **Confidence:** medium
