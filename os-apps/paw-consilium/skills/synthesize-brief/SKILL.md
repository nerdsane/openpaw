# Synthesize Brief

How to read N perspective outputs and synthesize a Brief, surfacing emergent insights and tensions.

## Process

1. **Read all perspective outputs** — fetch each from paw-fs using the provided file IDs
2. **Map the landscape** — for each perspective, note its role, lens, key claims, and recommendations
3. **Find emergent patterns**:
   - Where do multiple perspectives converge from different angles? (strong signal)
   - Where do perspectives directly conflict? (genuine tension worth surfacing)
   - What does combining perspectives reveal that no single one contains?
   - What blind spots exist across ALL perspectives?
4. **Synthesize, don't summarize**:
   - DO: "The technical and business perspectives converge on X, but for different reasons — this suggests..."
   - DON'T: "Perspective 1 said X. Perspective 2 said Y. Perspective 3 said Z."
5. **Structure the Brief**:
   - **Executive Summary** — 2-3 sentences capturing the most important synthesis
   - **Key Insights** — emergent findings from combining perspectives
   - **Tensions** — genuine disagreements with analysis of why they exist
   - **Convergences** — areas of agreement across different lenses
   - **Blind Spots** — what no perspective addressed
   - **Recommendations** — synthesized recommendations drawing from multiple viewpoints
   - **Perspective Inventory** — brief note on what each role contributed (for traceability)
6. **Create Brief entity** — write to paw-fs, create entity, publish

## Quality Standards

- The brief must contain insights that exist in NO single perspective
- Direct conflicts between perspectives must be preserved, not smoothed over
- The synthesizer's own view must not dominate — synthesis means combining, not judging
- Keep it actionable — vague meta-observations about "diverse perspectives" add nothing
